Every limit below is a limit of the engine as it stands, not of your approach. Each is
cheap to hit and expensive to discover halfway through a build, so each one also says
what does work instead — and, where something is planned, points at the one place
promises live: [the roadmap](/roadmap/).

## Hosting

### No dedicated server

Hosting means running the game as the host. The session ends when you leave, so you
cannot start one on a machine and leave it running.

**Instead:** host from the game, with `--become-host`. Your configuration survives: an
instance is described by `config/server.toml`, and a dedicated server changes which
process reads that file, not the file — see [Configuration](/server/configuration/).
Dedicated servers are committed → [Roadmap](/roadmap/).

## The mod surface

### No shooting, and no hitting a player with a ray

`spatial::raycast` deliberately excludes character bodies. Rays see map geometry and
the collision proxies of archetypes flagged `interact` or `contact` — never a player.
There is also no health anywhere in the engine.

So: no hitscan weapons, no melee that resolves against a player, no deathmatch.

**Instead:** a ray toward a player's position tells you whether the map occludes them
— enough for line of sight, hide-and-seek and vision cones; the `watchman` reference
mod is built on exactly that. And a shape probe is not a ray: `spatial::intersections`
reports every participant the probe touches, by session id — presence in a volume,
not a hit point.

### Proximity, not player-to-player touch

Contact events fire for archetypes you flagged, and tell you which participant touched
one of them. There is no player-to-player contact event, because neither party is a
flagged entity.

**Instead:** ask `spatial::intersections` with a probe around a point — every
participant it catches comes back by session id, which is what a tag zone needs. For
distances, the bundled `freeroam` gamemode publishes a player-positions fact over the
signal bus, and the `watchman` reference mod ranges on exactly that.

### Elimination has no spectator

There is one camera and it is a child of the body, so a player without a body sees
black rather than the world. A gamemode that spawned the body may despawn it — nothing
stops you leaving a player with nothing to look through.

**Instead:** "eliminated" has to mean *moved*. Teleport them to a pen, a balcony, a
jail. It plays better anyway, because they can watch and heckle.

### UI is one line of text

The whole client-side presentation surface is `ui::set-overlay-text`. No panels, no
images, no layout, no input widgets.

**Instead:** a scoreboard is a formatted string. A real UI surface is intended →
[Roadmap](/roadmap/).

### No items, no attachments

There is no inventory, no held item, and no way to attach one entity to another.

**Instead:** a "carried" object is one you move to follow its owner each tick.

### Nothing a mod writes survives the session

There is no storage of any kind. When the session ends, everything your mod knew is
gone — scores, unlocks, who built what, where they left it.

**Instead:** there is no workaround. A mod cannot reach a file or a network, so
nothing can be smuggled out. Build things that are true for one session. Persistence
is intended, and its shape is stated once, on the [roadmap](/roadmap/).

### No personas

A player, their account and their body are currently one and the same thing. One
account, one character, one body, always — so no character selection, no alt
characters, and no permadeath that costs a character rather than an account.

The contract already carries the split: a mod can ask a player for its profile — the
server-local persona — today, and the answer is none for everyone, so a mod written
now keys correctly when personas arrive.

**Instead:** treat the player as the character. It is the only shape available.
Personas are intended → [Roadmap](/roadmap/).

### Sound is one-shots only

You can ship a sound and play it at a volume you choose, and every listener hears it
the same wherever they stand → [Sound](/modding/presentation/sound/).

What does not exist is sound that has a place: a source anchored to an entity,
audible by distance, still playing when someone joins halfway through. Nor looping or
continuous sound of any kind, nor voice from a mod.

**Instead:** one-shots keyed to things that happen. A press, an impact, a pickup.

### No timers, no scheduling, no randomness

Your tick receives a delta, and every hook's context carries two tick numbers:
`raised-at`, the tick the event happened on, and `now`, the tick the handler runs on.
That is the whole time surface — you cannot ask for the wall clock, ask to be called
back later, or get a random number from the host.

**Instead:** accumulate the delta yourself for anything time-based, and count in
ticks where ordering matters — `raised-at` is the tick, so two events sort without
any clock. For randomness, seed a generator inside your own mod; just be aware that
nothing makes it agree with any other peer.

### The map answers one question

You can ask a loaded map for its spawn points. That is the whole surface.

There are no zones, no lookup of entities the map declares by name, no surface tags on
a raycast hit, no navmesh or pathfinding, no reading or overriding gravity, fog or sky.

**Instead:** put your own geometry in the world as archetypes and use contact events
and [spatial queries](/modding/world/spatial/) against those.

### The structural hooks are declared, and unfired

The contract declares a set of structural hooks — an entity spawned, a map loaded, a
body requested — each answering allow or deny. They are declared so a mod can already
export them, and unfired: the mechanism that orders mods and acts on the verdicts
does not exist yet, so today a mod observes other mods and does not take part in
their decisions.

**Instead:** cooperate by convention over [signals](/modding/messaging/signals/) and
[requests](/modding/messaging/requests/), and accept that the mod that owns a
behaviour has the last word on it. Composition hooks are intended →
[Roadmap](/roadmap/).

### You cannot ask whether a handle is still good

There is no way to test an entity handle for validity. A handle to something despawned
fails when you use it.

**Instead:** give your entities ids with `set-id` and fetch them with `by-id` each
time, rather than holding a handle across ticks.

### Cross-mod reach is identity and bytes, not handles

You cannot get a handle to another mod's entity. `find` and `by-id` are scoped to your
own ids, a ray that hits a foreign entity reports geometry without a handle, and an
intersections probe names a foreign entity by its full id — still no handle. Mods
cooperate by exchanging payloads over [signals](/modding/messaging/signals/) and
[requests](/modding/messaging/requests/).

This one is a deliberate boundary rather than a gap, and it is not going to be lifted:
a handle is permission to mutate.

## What this leaves

More than it sounds: race and parkour courses, king-of-the-hill, tag and infection,
hide-and-seek with real occlusion, red-light/green-light, floor-is-lava, puzzle rooms,
trigger-driven traps — anything built from moving props, per-player text and the keys
you bind.

The common shape is: watch positions, move things, recolour things, tell people what
happened.
